User persona

Based on three face-to-face interviews with users aged 22–24 testing the Milestone 3 wireframe, we put together one persona that represents them. Tags like [Interview 1] show which interview a point comes from.

A

Ana

Young adults who want to get more out of the clothes they own

  • Age: 23 (the three interviewees were 22–24)
  • Occupation: Student / junior employee
  • Location: București, Romania
  • Device: Smartphone, used for almost everything
  • Segment: Young adults who want to get more out of the clothes they own

About Ana

Ana is a young adult who likes to look put together but doesn’t want to spend time on it. She is comfortable with apps and expects them to make sense immediately. She skims forms and screens: if the main action isn’t obvious, she moves past it (“I’ll just click Next and not read” [Interview 1]). She is curious about new tech, but only if it is fast and effortless.

Attitude towards technology

Open to new things and quick to like useful features: Andra called the virtual try-on and Shop suggestions “really cool” and said she would use the app [Interview 1]. Her patience is short, though. When the app is unclear, she gets confused or moves on rather than exploring.

Goals

  • Get outfit ideas that fit the weather, the season and the occasion (work, casual, party) [Interview 1, Interview 2, Interview 3]
  • Reuse outfits she has already put together, grouped by type, e.g. “Weekend casual” [Interview 1]
  • Organise her outfits the way she does in everyday life, by category [Interview 3]
  • Build her digital wardrobe quickly, without much effort [Interview 2, Interview 3]
  • Stay up to date with new pieces from brands she likes, without searching [Interview 1]
  • See how clothes look on her own body before trying them on [Interview 1]
  • Save time deciding what to wear in the morning

Frustrations

  • Slow setup: filming a video of each piece or of her body is more effort than she wants; she would rather take a few photos [Interview 2, Interview 3]
  • Unclear labels: she assumes a tab contains suggestions when it contains her own outfits [Interview 2, Interview 3]
  • Key actions that are easy to miss: a small button gets skipped [Interview 1]
  • Not knowing what to do when scanning clothes: she wonders whether she has to wear the piece [Interview 2, Interview 3]
  • A changing body: her weight fluctuates (even through the month), so typed-in measurements go out of date and she prefers to scan herself again [Interview 1]
  • Pieces she can’t try: she finds something she likes online but can’t test it if the shop isn’t in the app [Interview 2]
  • Forgetting what she owns, so some clothes are never worn

Behaviours and habits

  • Expects her own wardrobe first when opening a wardrobe app, not suggestions [Interview 1]
  • Thinks in categories (casual, work, summer, winter...) and likes filters [Interview 1, Interview 3]
  • Prefers quick actions (photos, taps) over long processes [Interview 2, Interview 3]
  • Doesn’t follow every new release by herself and likes to be told [Interview 1]
  • Shops in well-known brands (H&M, Zara, Cropp, Pull&Bear)

Motivations

  • Saving time and effort every day
  • Getting value from clothes she already owns
  • Confidence in how she looks
  • Trying new styles without risk

What she expects from EasyFits

  • A setup that takes minutes, not an afternoon [Interview 2, Interview 3]
  • Suggestions that adapt to weather, season and occasion [Interview 1, Interview 2, Interview 3]
  • A clear difference between suggested outfits and her own outfits [Interview 2, Interview 3]
  • Her own organisation system (categories) respected [Interview 3]
  • A realistic view of clothes on her body, including when it changes [Interview 1]
  • Being notified about new pieces from partner brands [Interview 1]

Use cases

Four use cases show how Ana moves through the screens for the main tasks of the app. Screen codes refer to the wireframe (E = Everyday use, F = First launch).

UC-01: Scan a piece of clothing

Preconditions: She is logged in and has allowed camera access.

Entry points: the Scan tab (E11) or, for a new user, the step “Scan your first piece” on the empty Home (F2).

Main flow

  1. On the bottom navigation she taps Scan (E11).
  2. She taps Scan a piece (E12).
  3. She reads the tip: “You don’t need to wear it.”
  4. She lays the item flat or hangs it up and fits it in the frame.
  5. She takes 3 photos: front, back, detail.
  6. She taps Confirm scan.
  7. On Review your piece (E13) she checks the 3D model, name, category and tags.
  8. She taps Save to wardrobe.

Alternative flows

  • 5a. A photo is blurry or badly framed → Retake (the current photo is taken again).
  • 5b. She prefers an existing photo → Gallery.
  • 7a. The 3D model looks wrong → Rescan (back to step 4).
  • Any step: Close cancels the scan and returns to E11.

Result

The piece appears in Home (E3) with its category and tags.

UC-02: Build my body model

Preconditions: She is logged in and has allowed camera access.

Entry points: the Scan tab (E11) or, for a new user, the step “Build your 3D model” on the empty Home (F2).

Main flow

  1. She taps Scan (E11) → Scan or build your model.
  2. On Build your model (E14) she taps Scan my body.
  3. She takes 3 photos, standing about 2 m away: front, side, back (E15).
  4. She taps Confirm scan.
  5. The 3D body model is created and shown.

Alternative flows

  • 2a. She doesn’t want to scan → she enters height, chest, waist, hips and taps Save my sizes.
  • 3a. A photo is not good → Retake.
  • Later, her body changes → she repeats the scan to update the model.

Result

Her model is saved and used in try-on (E5, E18) and in outfits (E10).

UC-03: Create an outfit

Preconditions: She has at least a few pieces in her wardrobe.

Main flow

  1. She opens Outfits (E9) and taps + New.
  2. In the Outfit builder (E10) she chooses a top (step 1 of 4), then Next: Bottoms.
  3. She repeats for bottoms, shoes and coats. Each step can be skipped (“No top”).
  4. She sees the outfit on her 3D model.
  5. She taps Save outfit.
  6. She gives the outfit a name and picks one or more categories.

Alternative flows

  • 1a. She has no outfits yet → the Outfits tab shows Outfits (empty) (F4); she taps Create outfit and continues at step 2.
  • 3a. She has no pieces for a step → she skips it.
  • 5a. She leaves before saving → Back, nothing is saved.

Result

The outfit appears in “Outfits you created” (E9), where the filter chips (All, Casual, Work, Party, Sport, Summer, Winter) help her find outfits by category.

UC-04: See today’s suggestion

Preconditions: She has pieces in her wardrobe.

Main flow

  1. She opens Suggestions (E7).
  2. She sees “Today’s suggestion” on her 3D model, with a short reason why it works.
  3. She browses More ideas.
  4. She optionally opens the Style guide (E8).

Alternative flows

  • 1a. Her wardrobe is empty → Suggestions (empty) (F3) with the button Scan a piece.
  • 2a. She wants to change a piece → she taps Customise outfit, swaps it in the outfit builder (E10) and can save it with Save outfit.

Result

She knows what to wear today.

What we changed

Based on the interviews, we made six changes to the wireframe:

  1. The wardrobe is now the home screen. Opening the app takes you straight to your clothes.
  2. Outfit suggestions got their own tab. The old home screen (today’s suggestion, more ideas and the style guide) moved to the second tab, where the wardrobe used to be, and is now called Suggestions.
  3. “My outfits” is now “Outfits you created”, so it is clear these are your own outfits and not the suggested ones.
  4. Scanning uses photos instead of video. Clothes and the user’s body are scanned from a few guided photos (front, back, side) instead of recording a 360° video. When scanning a piece, a tip reminds you that you don’t need to wear it: just lay it flat or hang it up and fit it in the frame.
  5. Outfits can be filtered by category. “Outfits you created” now has filter chips (Casual, Work, Party, Sport, Summer, Winter), a subset of the tags used in the wardrobe.
  6. “Scan my body” is easier to find. It used to be a small toggle at the top of the body model screen. It is now the main button, centred at the bottom of the screen, where it is easier to see and reach.
