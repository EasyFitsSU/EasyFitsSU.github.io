Problem identification

Initially, we wanted to find a simple issue that almost everybody deals with and that can be solved. After thinking of various problems that people face on a daily basis, we came to the conclusion that outfit planning and getting dressed can easily become a time-consuming activity, whether we're talking about people who have no particular interest in fashion, or those who are passionate about it. Getting dressed can become particularly frustrating and time-consuming whenever we are testing outfits, especially when we cannot find the styling that we like. It goes hand in hand with outfit planning: painting an image of an outfit in our minds just to try it on and realise that we don't like it. Besides, almost everyone deals with the questions "How would this look on me?", "How would those clothes look in an outfit?" and "What goes well with this?".

Initial solution

After having thought of the issue and asking the questions above, we came up with a solution which answers the questions: EasyFits. The main characteristic of EasyFits, and which makes our platform unique, lies in a 3D model built on top of the silhouette of the user, which can act as a mannequin for the user to try on different outfits virtually. This not only saves a significant amount of time, but it also offers a clear understanding of what clothes and entire outfits would look like on the user, without having to actually try anything on in real life. We want the model to be free of generative AI and purely rely on machine learning and computer vision techniques in order to render every aspect: from the user's silhouette to the actual items of clothing that the user can virtually try on. Moreover, EasyFits generates outfit recommendations based on the user's virtual wardrobe. For example, one may not know about a potential combination of grey and burgundy from their own digital wardrobe, that they can wear for a more luxurious-looking outfit.

Customer discovery plan

For the customer discovery step, we wanted to validate whether the problems we identified in relation to outfit planning and clothing management are experienced by our potential customers, how frequently and how big of an impact they have on their daily lives. Our initial hypotheses are that people may experience difficulties when choosing outfits, combining clothing items they already own, keeping track of their wardrobe, and deciding whether to purchase clothing online. We also want to understand how people currently deal with these situations and whether existing methods or applications adequately address their needs.

To investigate these hypotheses, we used two different research methods:
1. Online questionnaire: we distributed a Google Forms questionnaire to collect responses from a broader group of potential customers. The questions focus on clothing habits, wardrobe organization, outfit selection and online shopping experiences. The results help with identifying recurring behaviors and patterns, as well as potential problem areas.
2. Face-to-face interviews: We conducted two semi-structured interviews with selected participants to gain a deeper understanding of their experience. The questions are asked in a way that aims for the participants to describe specific situations they encountered, without inducing the idea that there should be, in fact, a problem.
