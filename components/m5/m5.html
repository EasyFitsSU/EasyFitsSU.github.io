Lead generation campaign

From to we shared the EasyFits landing page through WhatsApp, Discord and Reddit to collect emails from people who want to hear when the app launches.

How we collect leads

A sign-up form at the bottom of the landing page asks for an email address. The email goes to a Google Form, so the list of leads stays with us and never reaches the analytics tool.

How we measure

PostHog (EU servers) records visits, clicks on the call to action, sign-ups and interactions with the 3D demo. It runs without cookies, so visitors don’t need to accept a consent banner. Every shared link carries a utm_source tag, so we know which channel each visitor came from. Our own team’s visits and localhost are excluded from all numbers.

Best channel

WhatsApp delivered 33 of our 59 visitors (about 56%) and 12 of our 17 sign-ups. Its 36.4% signup rate is well above the average of the other channels. Sharing directly with people we know meant high trust, and visitors engaged deeply with the demo: 73% of them played with it.

WhatsApp

Our strongest channel by a wide margin, for both traffic and sign-ups. We would keep using it, but it only reaches people close to us, so it will not scale on its own.

Discord

Only 5 visitors, but 3 signed up (60%), the best rate of any channel. It suggests the audience there matches the product. We would test it again with more posts before drawing conclusions.

Reddit

Almost no traffic and no sign-ups. Our test showed that a post without the link was accepted and was removed instantly once the link was added, so the link was the clear trigger.

Direct / other

19 visitors with 2 sign-ups (10.5%) and 4 demo interactions. They engaged the least of any group, which suggests that a personal message or a post gives much better context than a bare link.

Reddit in detail

What we did

Reddit was the channel where we hit the most problems, so we are documenting the whole process. Every post was written for its own subreddit and followed that community’s rules (format, flair, self-promotion). We still could not get the link to stay up.

  1. Account 1. We posted tailored posts with the landing page link. They were removed by Reddit’s filters and the account was banned.
  2. Account 2. A new account, same approach, same result: removed by the filters and banned.
  3. Account 3: a test. We posted a normal post without the GitHub link. It was published and everything was fine. About 5 minutes later we added a comment with the link. As soon as the link appeared, the filter flagged it and the whole post was removed with “Sorry, this post was removed by Reddit’s filters”.

In total we made more than 15 posts, and only one became public. Across the campaign, about 80% of cases showed the “removed by Reddit’s filters” message, which means the automatic spam filter blocked the post, not the subreddit moderators. In the other 20% the post was waiting to be approved.

What the test on account 3 shows

It is the closest thing we have to a controlled experiment. Same account, same post, same subreddit, a few minutes apart. The only difference was the link.

  • Without the link, the post was accepted and published.
  • With the link, it was flagged instantly and the entire post was removed, not just the comment.

This is strong evidence that the link was what triggered the filter, and not the topic, the wording, the format or the subreddit rules. Because the removal was instant, it points to an automatic check on the link and not to a person reading the post.

Why a link can be treated as suspicious

Reddit does not publish how its spam filter works, but there is evidence that it filters by link or domain:

  • Reports on Reddit’s sitewide filter describe posts with certain domains being hidden, some of them “soft-filtered” so that moderators can approve them manually, with no explanation given to the user.
  • Other founders and developers have reported the same behavior for their own sites, for example posts with dev.to links being blocked while posts without them were fine.
  • Reddit has also banned whole domains in the past because of repeated spam or vote cheating, and it discourages accounts that mostly submit links to a site they own or benefit from.

Our own assumption is that github.io is flagged because free hosting domains are often used for spam and phishing, and that a new account posting a link to its own project adds to the suspicion. We could not confirm this with Reddit, and we do not know whether github.io itself, the utm parameters or the combination with a new account is the exact cause.

What we would do differently

  • Use a custom domain instead of github.io, without utm parameters in the visible link, and check it before posting.
  • Use one real account and build it up with useful comments for a few weeks before sharing anything.
  • Post without the link first and ask the moderators (modmail) if a link is allowed, so they can approve it manually.
  • Offer the link on request (comments or direct messages) in communities that allow it.

Session replays

We watched the recorded sessions in PostHog to see how visitors use the page.

  • People like to play. Many visitors spent their time trying out the animation and testing different things on the page.
  • Moving the model is the catchiest part. Being able to rotate the 3D model around was what grabbed most visitors’ attention.
  • Only some want to know more. A part of the visitors also looked at how the app works, but not all of them did.

Takeaways

  • Personal sharing worked best. Sending the link directly (WhatsApp) beat posting in public communities (Reddit) for a very early-stage product.
  • Reddit did not work, and the link was the trigger. Of more than 15 tailored posts, only one became public. On our third account, a post without the link was accepted and was removed by the filters as soon as we added the link. Two visitors and no sign-ups.
  • Account workarounds are not a solution. Two accounts were banned, and opening a third to investigate goes against Reddit’s rules on multiple accounts and ban evasion. Reddit needs a real account history and a trusted link, which takes weeks.
  • Discord is promising. The signup rate was the highest (60%), but 5 visitors is too few to be sure.
