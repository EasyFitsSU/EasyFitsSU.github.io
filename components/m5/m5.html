Lead generation campaign

From to we shared the EasyFits landing page through WhatsApp, Discord and Reddit to collect emails from people who want to hear when the app launches.

How we collect leads

A sign-up form at the bottom of the landing page asks for an email address. The email goes to a Google Form, so the list of leads stays with us and never reaches the analytics tool.

How we measure

PostHog (EU servers) records visits, clicks on the call to action, sign-ups and interactions with the 3D demo. It runs without cookies, so visitors don’t need to accept a consent banner. Every shared link carries a utm_source tag, so we know which channel each visitor came from. Our own team’s visits and localhost are excluded from all numbers.

“Direct / other” are visitors who arrived without a tracked source, for example by opening a link copied from a chat or typing the address.

Best channel

WhatsApp delivered 32 of our 53 visitors (about 60%) and 11 of our 15 sign-ups. Its 34.4% signup rate is well above the average of the other channels. Sharing directly with people we know meant high trust, and visitors engaged deeply with the demo: 72% of them played with it.

WhatsApp

Our strongest channel by a wide margin, for both traffic and sign-ups. We would keep using it, but it only reaches people close to us, so it will not scale on its own.

Discord

Only 5 visitors, but 3 signed up (60%), the best rate of any channel. It suggests the audience there matches the product. We would test it again with more posts before drawing conclusions.

Reddit

Almost no traffic and no sign-ups, mostly because our posts never became public. Reddit’s spam filters removed most of them. We would build up the account first and try again.

Direct / other

11 visitors with 1 sign-up (9.1%) and no demo interactions. They engaged the least of any group, which suggests that a personal message or a post gives much better context than a bare link.

What went wrong on Reddit

We made more than 15 posts and only one became public. In about 80% of cases Reddit showed “Sorry, this post was removed by Reddit’s filters”, which means the automatic spam filter blocked it, not the subreddit moderators. In the other 20% the post was waiting to be approved. The most likely reasons:

  • New account or low karma. Accounts with no history of comments and posts are treated with suspicion, especially when they post a link.
  • The link. github.io is often used for spam and phishing, so filters flag it easily, and the utm parameters make it look more suspicious.
  • The same link in many places. Posting the same text or link in several subreddits looks like spam, and a link used often can be flagged for later posts too.
  • Purely promotional posts. The account had no other activity, and its only posts linked to our own project.

Next time we would build up the account with normal comments first and use a custom domain instead of github.io.

Session replays

We watched the recorded sessions in PostHog to see how visitors use the page.

  • People like to play. Many visitors spent their time trying out the animation and testing different things on the page.
  • Moving the model is the catchiest part. Being able to rotate the 3D model around was what grabbed most visitors’ attention.
  • Only some want to know more. A part of the visitors also looked at how the app works, but not all of them did.

Takeaways

  • Personal sharing worked best. Sending the link directly (WhatsApp) beat posting in public communities (Reddit) for a very early-stage product.
  • Reddit did not work. Of more than 15 posts only one became public, because Reddit’s spam filters removed the rest. Two visitors and no sign-ups. A mistyped UTM tag also made the data harder to read.
  • Discord is promising. The signup rate was the highest (60%), but 5 visitors is too few to be sure.
  • Next time: double-check every UTM tag before posting, and test Discord and Reddit with more posts in more targeted communities.
